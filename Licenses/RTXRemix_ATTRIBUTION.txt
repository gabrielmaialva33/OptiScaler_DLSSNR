NVIDIA RTX Remix: DLSS-NR highlight recovery
============================================

Project:  RTX Remix runtime (dxvk-remix), NVIDIA
Source:   https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix, commit ecf646d3ea97b1e81e450993f414a2ae31b71d3b
          ("DLSS 3D-Guided Neural Generation Integration", 2026-09-18)

What is derived from it
-----------------------

The optional highlight recovery step in the DLSS Neural Rendering resolve
(OptiScaler/shaders/dlssnr/precompile/dlssnr.hlsl, [DlssNr] HighlightRecovery,
off by default) reimplements, in HLSL, the highlight recovery of RTX Remix's
inverse tone mapping pass:

  * src/dxvk/shaders/rtx/pass/tonemap/tonemapping_inverse_tonemapping.comp.slang,
    lines 84-106: a mask equal to a smoothstep on the exposed HDR frame's
    largest channel times a smoothstep on the largest channel of what the
    model was shown, used to blend the result back toward the original HDR
    frame;
  * src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_dlss_neural_rendering.h, lines 78-83: the default
    smoothstep edges (1.0, 8.0, 0.75, 0.99), used here as fixed constants.

What is not theirs: Remix applies the mask after an ACES tone map and a
per-channel inverse of it. Neither is used here. The exposed frame is the
pass's own normalised frame (the frame over its white point), and what the
model was shown is the pass's own encode curve (soft knee, Neutwo or Hybrid)
reproduced per pixel. The composition the mask is applied over is described in
OptiScaler/dlssnr/README.md and is not derived from Remix. The analysis is in
docs/dlssnr/design/hdr-highlight-recovery.md.
